### Behavior

- **Opening** (§7.3): Open call (card), Watch (card), a sharer's screen glyph. The surface grows out of the card
  it came from (`findCallCard`: your call's card for the channel, else any card for it, not one sliding out);
  with no card on screen it grows from 96% of itself, fading in. A fresh view opens with the chat column off.
- **Focus** (§7.2): the stage target's `streamerId` is the stream in focus. When the view is on the call and
  someone else shares, the earliest share comes into focus on its own; "Show everyone" puts it away for this
  view (it does not come back on its own). Clicking a sharer's tile focuses their stream; a camera tile focuses
  that camera (local state); your own share's tile shows your preview. Leaving stream focus stops the watch.
- **Closing** (§7.3): Back to chat / Esc. The view unmounts at once, so every camera and the stream unsubscribe
  and the watch (and its audio) stops; the voices continue. A still copy shrinks into the card. Not joined,
  closing stops watching. Leaving the call (the view's Leave, the card's, or the call ending) closes the view
  when nothing is being watched; joining from Watch keeps it open.
- **Theater** (§5.9): the host moves to the window's inset; the surface FLIPs from its old rect and the sidebar
  dims to .4 (IX-4's CSS). The chat column stays as it was; with it off, messages surface over the stream.
- **DM calls**: named for the person, "Direct call", the DM is the chat column. **Stage channels**: speakers are
  tiles, the audience one row; Request to speak; `StageChannelPanel` heads the chat column.
- **Phone**: the view fills the screen with no chat column and no theater (IX-16 makes it a sheet).

### Motion (ids `ix-call:*`, transform and opacity only)

| Moment | What plays |
|---|---|
| Open (§5.8) | `open`: translate + non-uniform scale from the card's rect, origin 0 0, opacity .3 → 1, 600ms spring. `open-content`: the counter-layer undoes the scale at 16 samples of the spring (linear between) and holds at 0 until 40%, then fades in |
| Back to chat | `close`: the copy shrinks to the card's rect, 450ms spring, to .3, then is removed; `close-content` fades the copy's content by 30% |
| Theater (§5.9) | `theater` 600ms spring from the old rect; `theater-content` keeps the content at its new size while the surface grows around it |
| Reduced motion | 150ms opacity fades |

Nothing fills forwards on anything that stays (the close copy is removed); nothing runs 2s after a move (e2e).

### Native video (§7.6)

`beginCallMove()` lays a `data-native-shield` over the window and calls `beginNativeMove()` for every move (and
250ms around an H change): tiles close their holes and show their posters (still decoding), the page's ground
is opaque, and the surface keeps its own fill (`html[data-native-moving] .ix-call`). While a hole is open the
surface is clear, so nothing beneath it is painted (main's content; in theater and on a phone the sidebar and its
hairline). The close copy carries each canvas's poster and none of the hole attributes. Controls, overlay and
popovers over video are near-solid (`--ix-pop-solid`, `color-mix(--ix-call 86%)`), never blurred.

## Decisions and deviations

1. **Flame and heart are not in the controls.** 1bc00a09 ("remove stream reaction controls and overlay") retired
   them from the Glass stage the morning the spec was approved, and `StreamReactionsLayer` is mounted nowhere;
   index-spec §7.2 lists them but also says reactions "keep their Glass behavior". I followed the code. If the
   owner wants them back it is two buttons in `CallControls` and mounting the layer in `StreamPicture`.
2. **Camera tiles are ported, not composed.** `CallTile` carries `CameraTile`'s exact video route (and the
   native-video branch's rules) with the Index look (88px avatar, Index name and wave); `StagePeople.tsx` goes
   with the Glass shells (IX-19).
3. **"Share quality" stays in More**, and your own preview is your tile (the Glass row had both as buttons).
4. **Stage mode stays `'side'`** in the store (the view says `data-mode="main"`); the rename waits for the Glass
   shells to go (M2.md, IX-19).
5. **The chat column is the call's own channel** (the voice channel's text, the DM); `SurfacingMessages` still
   surfaces the route's conversation (composed unchanged).
6. **Room card** (IX-7's file, one line): `CardActions.tsx` imports `DeviceMenu` from `ix/call/CallMenus`.
7. **Vocabulary** (the coordinator's rule): no user-facing "room" in the call view or the card (`vocabulary.test.ts`
   passes; I found none in `components/ix/card`).

**Not joined on the desktop, deliberately.** The desktop's microphone path opens ALSA's `pipewire` PCM for the
system default (`devices.rs` `default_target` → `server_pcm_index`), which the shipped
`99-pipewire-default.conf` pins to `capture_node "-1"`: the real default source, whatever `~/.asoundrc` says.
A nested-session join would have opened the owner's Scarlett. The joined path (s3, controls, Leave) is covered by
the mocked harness and the real-server spec (Chromium fake devices). The scene's headless Chromiums were
playing call audio to the default sink when first started; I moved them to the `glass_g23_out` null sink and
restarted the scene with `PULSE_SINK=glass_g23_out`. Future live runs should start the scene that way.

Every process I started was stopped (app, nested KWin, drive server, scene holder and its browsers, instance).
Note: the release `paracord-server` build replaced the shared `Paracord-glass/target/release/deps/paracord_server-*`
artifact (same hash; the previous one was another package's build); `target/release/paracord-desktop` was
re-linked to the Glass build as it was.
